An identity for Canary Palestine, built from the bird it is named after: it sings while the air is good, and its silence is the warning.

01THE IDEA

The listening post

Miners carried a canary underground because it noticed first. While it sang, the air was good. When it went quiet, everyone left. Canary Palestine listens to water, air, power and health the same way: station by station, report by report, and it treats a silence as news.

  1. 01

    Listen, then say it plainly

    Every station is one place and one thing: a village water line, a clinic network, a solar plant. Its status is the desk's reading of a named report, never a feeling about the day.

  2. 02

    Name the source, every time

    Each reading carries who said it, through whom, and when. A reading without a source is not shown.

  3. 03

    Count the silence

    When a station has had no public report, the desk does not fill the gap. It shows the gap, counts the days, and asks why.

02THE MARK

The c is the canary

The first letter of the name is drawn as the bird's head: round, with an eye on the crown and the beak open, tipped up the way a canary throws its head back to sing. In the wordmark the head sings its own name: the open beak faces «anary».

03THE CALLS

Four calls, four statuses

After the head comes its call, drawn the way a sonograph draws sound: time across, pitch up. The calls are the desk's status system. They hold their meaning without colour, so the site reads the same in print, in greyscale and to a reader who cannot tell yellow from white.

Song

STATUS · STEADY

Working, restored or added. A trill of rising notes, even and unbroken: the canary is singing, the air is good.

Strained

STATUS · WATCH

Damage, loss or a risk reported. The trill breaks: a note drops out, the last ones fall and shorten.

Alarm

STATUS · ALERT

Harm reported now. Short broadband chips in pairs, the shape an alarm call takes on a sonogram: tall, abrupt, the whole height of the band.

Silent

STATUS · SILENT

No recent report. No call, and the beak is shut. The desk never fills a silence with a guess; it counts the days.

The sounds are synthesised in the page, a few hundred milliseconds each, and play only when a reader presses a button.

04WORDMARK

canary, and where it listens

The wordmark is lowercase Geologica at 780 with its cursive forms switched on, so every a is single-storey and rounds off like the head. PALESTINE is set in Martian Mono, the instrument face, under «anar» and stopping short of the y: a station tag on a gauge.

Sizes

Below 120 pixels wide PALESTINE stops being legible: use the head alone. Clear space on every side is the head's eye, three times over.

Never

  • Redraw the head as a closed c or a full disc. The open beak and the eye are the mark.
  • Rotate the head so the beak points down or back. It sings forward, into the name.
  • Put the call after the wordmark as decoration. A call is a status; it is shown only where it means one.
  • Use canary for text on paper. On paper canary is a fill, under coal; text accents use canary ink #5E5300.

05COLOUR

Coal, canary, paper

The palette is the mine and the instrument. Coal is where the network is heard; sonograph paper is where reports are read. Canary is the bird, and like the bird it is only noticed when it matters: an alert, the lead, the silence. There is no second accent colour; status is carried by the shape of the call, not by red and green.

06TYPE

A voice that sharpens

Geologica sets the voice: headlines, reading, navigation. It has a sharpness axis, from soft rounded joins to cut corners, and the site uses it as a status channel: a steady report is set soft, a watch halfway, an alert sharp. Martian Mono sets the instruments: codes, readings, dates. Its width axis lets a reading condense to the width of its gauge.

07SONOGRAM

The month, as the network sounds

The homepage opens on the listening post: a sonogram of every report in the last 30 days. Each row is a station, each mark a report drawn as its call. Read across, a row is a station's month; read down, a column is a day on the network. Press play and the month is sung back, left to right, every station in its place in the stereo field.

  1. Bands are the lines: water, air & waste, power, health, like frequency bands on a sonogram.
  2. Rows are stations, coded by line (W1, A2, P3, H4) and named by place and subject.
  3. Marks are reports, drawn as the call of their status. Alerts alone are canary.
  4. A dotted bracket is a silence, from the last report to today, with its count in days.
  5. The lead is boxed, so the story at left can be found in the month at right.
  6. The grain is the paper, a faint static under everything, as on a real print.

08PHOTOGRAPHS

As they were taken

Most stories carry a photograph, shown in its own colour with no filter or tint, cropped only to fit its frame. The identity stays in the type, the calls and the colour around the picture, never on top of it. Where a station has no honest photograph, its card shows the station's call instead.

  • Only what it shows. A photo sits next to a story only if it shows that place or that kind of thing. The caption gives its real date and place, even when that is years before the report, and says so when it is a nearby stand-in.
  • Infrastructure, not suffering. Pumps, pipes, panels, clinics, landscapes. No injured people, no children in distress.
  • Free and credited. Wikimedia Commons, with author and licence under every picture.
  • No photograph, no pretending. A station without one shows its call on a plate and says "No photograph".

09MOTION & SOUND

It moves like it listens

Motion is the stylus and the bird, nothing else: lines draw left to right the way a sonograph writes, the head opens its beak before it sings. Everything settles fast and nothing loops while a reader is reading. With reduced motion requested, the page arrives still and complete.

  • The first call≈1.9 s · onceLoader, first visit per session only: the stylus line draws, the head pops in with its beak shut, throws its head back and sings its trill, then the board slides away. Skipped in background tabs, with reduced motion, and never held longer than 4.5 s.

  • Tooltips160 msA reading card follows a mark under the pointer or keyboard focus; it never covers the mark.

10APPLICATIONS

Out in the world

The same few pieces carry everywhere: the head, the call, a reading in Martian Mono, a sentence that names its source. An alert looks like an alert at any size, because its call does not depend on colour.

11VOICE

Say what was reported

The desk writes like a careful instrument: what was measured, where, by whom, when. Statuses are the desk's reading of named reports, not official alerts, and the copy never pretends otherwise.

  • In August, three in four Gaza water samples lacked the chlorine that keeps water safe.

    Gaza's water is poisoned.

    The number and the test, not a conclusion the report does not make.

  • No public air-quality reading for the Strip since 12 August.

    Air quality in Gaza is unknown but likely terrible.

    A silence is reported as a silence, with its date.

  • Health partners, via OCHA situation report of 25 Sep.

    Sources say.

    Who said it, through whom, and when, on every reading.

  • Back since 16 Sept after a 54-day breakdown.

    Great news for Nablus!

    Steady is a status, not a celebration.
